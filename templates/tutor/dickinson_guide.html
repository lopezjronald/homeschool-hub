Day 3

Choose the word she found most interesting and say why, then write a micro-story of about 150 words using as many of the week's words as it will carry. The guide also teaches a way of turning those sentences into a Dickinson-style poem — worth trying once she is comfortable, but the story is the assignment.

Why the copying is done by hand

The guide lists handwriting practice and contemplative attention to detail among the skills it teaches, and the copying is how it teaches them. A line copied at a keyboard practises neither — the point is to slow down enough to notice where Dickinson put her dashes.

So on this site:

  • Copy the word and the definition — {% if by_hand.copy_definition %}written by hand{% else %}typed{% endif %}
  • Copy Dickinson's lines — {% if by_hand.copy_example %}written by hand{% else %}typed{% endif %}
  • Craft a sentence of your own — {% if by_hand.own_sentence %}written by hand{% else %}typed{% endif %}
  • Day 3, the word she chose and the micro-story — {% if by_hand.micro_story %}written by hand{% else %}typed{% endif %}, because a 150-word story wants revising and the skill there is the writing rather than the letters

She writes with a finger or a stylus, and her handwriting is kept exactly as she formed it — it appears in the work browser and on the printed charter report. The AI grader is told plainly that it cannot read handwriting and must leave those to you.

What to look for when you check it

  • The copying: every word, every dash, every capital as Dickinson wrote them. Her punctuation is not decoration — the em dashes are the poem breathing.
  • Her own sentence: does it use the word the way it actually means? A sentence that would work just as well with any other word in that slot has not used it.
  • The micro-story: does it carry the week's words without straining, and still read as a story?

A note on the printed guide

Week 16 prints its headword as perrenial. The standard spelling is perennial — Dickinson's own letter reads “perrennial”, which the guide marks [sic] in the example but not in the headword. Her page says so, because she is copying it out by hand and should not learn it wrong. Week 8 prints Habiliment capitalised where every other headword is lower case; that one is left as printed.
