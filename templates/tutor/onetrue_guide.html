How a week runs

Each week is two pages, and here that is two things to turn in.

  1. The lesson. Read the explanation and its example. Copy Sentence 1 — a real sentence from a Caldecott Award winning book — by hand. Read it silently, then again slowly in a whisper. Then answer the questions about Sentence 2 and Sentence 1: where is the tool, what is it doing, why did the author reach for it?
  2. Now you try! Her own sentences using the same tool. Usually five; some weeks split into smaller groups (three with you're and three with your), and two weeks are paired — write a fragment then make it whole, write a run-on then break it up.

Which parts she writes by hand

  • Copying Sentence 1 — {% if by_hand.copy_sentence %}written by hand{% else %}typed{% endif %}. The point of putting a real author's sentence in front of her is that she writes it out slowly enough to notice how it is built.
  • The noticing questions — {% if by_hand.notice %}written by hand{% else %}typed{% endif %}. They are short, often a word or a phrase, and typing them means the grader can read them and tell you whether she actually found the device.
  • Her own sentences — {% if by_hand.own_sentences %}written by hand{% else %}typed{% endif %}. She is a slow typist; five sentences hunted out on a keyboard is an endurance test with nothing to do with sentence craft.

Handwritten answers are kept exactly as she formed them and appear in the work browser and the printed charter report. The AI grader is told plainly that it cannot read handwriting and must leave those to you.
